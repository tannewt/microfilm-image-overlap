# The Parkers of the Galesville Newspaper OCR

A summary of every mention of the **Parker** surname found in the OCR results
(`ocr_results/paddle/` — PaddleOCR output over the Galesville, Wisconsin newspaper scans
in `20260826-newspaper-scans/`). Nearly 800 raw hits were found across the corpus as
OCR has progressed; most are either the recurring **Larson & Parker** livery
advertisement or syndicated national news/fiction. The Galesville-area individuals are
covered first.

Volume dates: `P81-4720` ≈ 1895–96 (masthead "FRIDAY, JANUARY 18, 1895" survives on
page_0134-0135), `P81-4721` ≈ 1896–97, `P81-4722` ≈ 1900 (masthead "FRIDAY, JUNE 22,
1900" on page_0112-0113).

**OCR coverage (final):** the corpus is fully OCR'd — all 2,120 pages across the
three rolls (P81-4720: 673 pp., ~1895–96; P81-4721: 658 pp., ~1896–97;
P81-4722: 789 pp., ~1897–1900; rolls are year-mixed bindings, so dates
interleave). No further pages are pending.

## Galesville-area Parkers

### 1. William H. (W. H.) Parker — architect, of La Crosse and Galesville

The most-mentioned Parker by far. He was **"the well-known La Crosse architect"** who
bought property in Galesville and moved there:

> "W. H. Parker, the La Crosse architect, has secured this magnificent property for a
> snug sum, and will soon remove to Galesville to live. Mr. Parker will expend a
> thousand or so in putting the place in good shape." (headline "GALE PROPERTY SOLD")

Other evidence:

- He had ties to **Gale College**: he appears in a roll call of the college's "former
  instructors" ("…Mrs. W. H. Parker, Mr. and Mrs. W. A. Tower, Prof. and Mrs. L. S.
  Kerth…"), and later "W. H. Parker will assist in giving the instruction" to a
  chemistry class organized in Gale College.
- As an architect he "draw[s] the plans for the new block" (P81-4720, page_0079-0080)
  and "Architect Parker has drawn the plans" for a local building; "Plans submitted to
  the building committee by Architect Parker" (1896); the church "drawing made by
  Architect Parker" for the frame residence/church at Ninth and Main streets, whose
  foundation work began the previous fall (P81-4721, page_0499-0501). He also employed
  help: "John Twomey, an employee of W. H. Parker, was thrown from a wagon Wednesday
  and the result was a broken arm."
- As a family man: "W. H. Parker and daughters spent a Monday in La Crosse"; "Mrs.
  W. H. Parker and her two little girls returned Saturday after several [weeks]";
  his mother visited him here and "left Wednesday morning for Faribault, Minn."; and
  "W. H. Parker accompanied his mother to Chicago this week, she being on her return
  to Ogdensburg, N. Y." (May 1896) — so the family's roots lie back east. "Mrs. Parker
  and Laura B. Russell, of Galesville, were in Glasgow Friday."
- **Clover Ridge Farm is the Parker home**: "invitations issued by Mrs. W. H. Parker,
  a score of ladies assembled at Clover Ridge Farm … The spacious dining room of the
  **Parker mansion** had been prettily decorated" (1896); "Mrs. Worthington, of
  Chicago, is a guest at the Parker mansion" (the cousin, now of Chicago). Several
  fragments read "leave word at Clover Ridge farm … William H. Parker, Galesville,
  Wis." — suggesting the Clover Ridge correspondence items were signed by him
  (tentative; columns interleaved).
- Later items treat him as a settled local: "W. H. Parker is home from his western
  trip. He enjoyed the outing immensely" (P81-4721, page_0123); "William H. Parker,
  Galesville, Wis." is given as a contact address in a want ad (P81-4722,
  page_0589-0590); "the road between the village and Mrs W. H. Parker's place" (1900);
  and "W. H. Parker accompanied his mother to Chicago this week, she being on her
  return to Ogdensburg, N. Y." (May 1896).

### 3. M. B. Parker — merchant on Allen Street

> "M. B. Parker has moved his family into the rooms over his store building on Allen
> street."

> "Miss M. B. Parker is visiting friends in Greenwood, Wis." *(likely "Mrs.")*

> "Mrs. Richard Towner, of Trempealeau, was the guest of Mrs. M. B. Parker Tuesday."

Also: a reception was held "at the home of M. B. Parker"; listed as "M B Parker" among
finishers in a bicycle race; "M. B. Parker was in the city Tuesday"; "M. B. Parker has
been quite ill for several days" and later "has sufficiently recovered"; "Mrs. M. B.
Parker visited in Trem[pealeau]"; "Mrs. M. B. Parker and daughter, Emma, are visiting
relatives at Green[wood]"; a gentleman "of St. Lawrence, S.D., … [was] the guest of
M. B. Parker, of whom the gentleman is a relative." He was a **railroad man**:
"Breakman M. B. Parker" — in April 1898, while coupling cars, "his hand was caught

"M. B. Parker resumed his duties on the railroad Monday after a short vacation" and

"arrived in the city Tuesday and will spend a week with his parents"** and "Mrs. Ed
Parker and children, of Greenwood, were the guests of M. B. Parker" — suggesting Ed is
M. B. Parker's son.

### 5. Erving (Irving/Irve) Parker — young local, bicycle racer, married 1895

> "Erving Parker and Miss Minnie Nilsestuen were married Tuesday evening, March 26, at
> the home of the groom's parents in this city, Rev. J. W. Winder officiating. The
> wedding was a quiet [affair] … Mr. and Mrs. Parker will settle down [on the] farm, a
> couple of miles east of the city."

This confirms the earlier finds:

> "M. Parker and Irving Parker have bought the Wright property on the [—]" *(likely
> "M. B. Parker and Irving Parker" — and the bride married "at the home of the groom's
> parents in this city")*

> "Irve Parker had business at Winona Tuesday."

Also listed ("Irvi Parker") among finishers in the same bicycle race as M. B. Parker.
The 1895 marriage (Rev. J. W. Winder was Galesville's Presbyterian pastor) and the
farm "a couple of miles east of the city" match the Wright-property purchase. The
spelling varies in the OCR: Erving / Irving / Irve — same man. Deed records confirm
the "Ervin" spelling: "Duncan Wright to Ervin Parker et al, Mar 11, '95, part lot 4
block 4 Galesville, $450."

### 10. Larson & Parker — Livery, Feed and Sale Stable

The single largest source of "Parker" hits (~90 of 208) is the recurring display ad
**"LARSON & PARKER, Livery, Feed and Sale Stable — Good rigs and drivers. Commercial
trade a specialty. Galesville, Wis."** The other partner is **Ole E. Larson**. Editorial
mentions confirm the firm:

> "Larson & Parker, the liverymen, are repainting their rigs and otherwise pre-
> paring…"

> "It is said that Larson & Parker, the liverymen, will erect a large and convenient
> stable during the coming season."

> "Larson & Parker did a rushing business in the bus line."

> "…purchased Saturday from the stables of Larson & Parker."

The identity of the Parker partner is **not stated** in the OCR — possibly Irving
Parker (see #5), but that is conjecture. One of the firm's livery horses ("one of the
span of grays owned by Larson & Parker") died in May 1896.

## 11. George Parker, of Humbird, Wis. — M. B. Parker's brother, born in Vermont

> "News reached this city yesterday of the death of George Parker, whose demise
> occurred at Humbird, Wis., on the 26th inst. Deceased was well known here and was a
> **brother of M. B. Parker, Mrs. William Truax and Mrs. John McWain**. He was born
> in **Vermont, Sept. 14, 1847**. The remains will be interred tomorrow (Saturday) at
> Centerville."

A pivotal item: it shows the Galesville Parkers were **siblings** — M. B. Parker, Mrs.
William Truax (born a Parker — see `TRUAXES.md`), **Mrs. John McWain (see
`MCWAIN.md`)**, and George. It
also shows the family came from **Vermont** — the strongest Vermont connection found
in the corpus. George lived at Humbird, Wis. and was buried at Centerville (next to
Galesville).

Source: `ocr_results/paddle/P81-4721/page_0421-0422.json`

## Caveats

- Quotes are verbatim OCR and frequently garbled; several pages (the `_ambiguous/`
  set) have interleaved columns, so attributions marked "tentative" may belong to a
  neighboring article.
- Family relationships are now partially documented: the George Parker obituary
  (1896) names **M. B. Parker, Mrs. William Truax, Mrs. John McWain and George
  Parker as siblings**, and states the family was born in Vermont. Whether Emma,
  Erving, Ed or Dr. W. E. Parker belong to M. B. Parker's household is still not
  stated outright (Ed's visits "with his parents" and his family staying "with
  M. B. Parker" suggest he is M. B. Parker's son). Erving Parker married at "the
  home of the groom's parents in this city."
- The Parker of "Larson & Parker" is never given a first name in the OCR.
- The earlier, lower-quality OCR pass under
  `20260826-newspaper-scans/P81-472*/ocr_results/` was not used; its output is too
  garbled to add reliable mentions.
