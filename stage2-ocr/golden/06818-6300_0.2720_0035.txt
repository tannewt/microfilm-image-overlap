CENTERVILLE.
Special to The Independent.
May 15.—Last Friday evening a bur-
glar entered E. R. Johnson's store and
carried away about $15 or $20 worth of
goods. He gained an entrance by
breaking a sash lock and prying up a
window that was nailed down. On
Monday Mr. Johnson received informa-
tion which led him to suspect or rather
to confirm the suspicion that Joel Smith
was his man. Accordingly he went
down to Justice Gibson and swore out
a search warrant and placed in the
hands of James Merwin, who at once
proceeded to the place where Smith
was working, and commenced work. A
part of the goods were soon found and
when Joe saw he was caught, he fished
out the balance of the goods and ac-
companied Merwin over to the Justice
office, where he plead guilty and was
bound over. Tuesday morning he was
taken to Whitehall where he will re-
ceive his sentence from the Judge. It
is quite certain that he will wear a
striped suit one year and perhaps
more. Johnson would like to know
now who borrowed a rolling coulter
and some barb wire from him last fall.
This will no doubt be followed up to a
finish. This is not the first time Smith
has stolen things. Last fall he stole a
nice overcoat from Leterski at Dodge,
but settled the matter without arrest.
He has also taken other thing too num-
erous to mention. The goods stolen
from the store were two knives, two
mouth organs, half a dozen shirts, box
cigars, overalls, suspenders, mittens,
six pair men's socks and one pair of
ladies' hose. Evidently Joe was not a
selfish fellow and made provision for a
lady friend. This should be a warning
to others.
