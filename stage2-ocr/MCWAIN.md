# The McWains of the Galesville Newspaper OCR

A summary of the **McWain** (also OCR'd as "MeWain") surname mentions in the OCR
results (`ocr_results/paddle/` — PaddleOCR output over the Galesville, Wisconsin
newspaper scans). 35 raw hits, nearly all local.

**OCR coverage (final):** the corpus is fully OCR'd — all 2,120 pages across the
three rolls (P81-4720: 673 pp., ~1895–96; P81-4721: 658 pp., ~1896–97;
P81-4722: 789 pp., ~1897–1900; rolls are year-mixed bindings, so dates
interleave). No further pages are pending.

**Key connection:** the George Parker death notice (Feb. 1896) names "Mrs. John
McWain" as a **sister of M. B. Parker, George Parker, and Mrs. William Truax** — she
was born a **Parker**, and the Parker siblings came from Vermont (see `PARKERS.md`,
`VERMONT.md`). The McWain obituary below confirms her husband John McWain and lists
their children.

---

## 1. John McWain — family patriarch

Named in his son William's obituary as "a son of Mr. and Mrs. John McWain." His wife
(born a Parker) survived William. The couple had **five children**, of whom at
William's death (1896) the survivors were William's brothers **Everett and Bert**;
"two sisters died long ago" — so Mrs. John McWain's known children were:
**William (b. 1865), Everett, Bert, and two sisters who died young** (Miss Effie and
Miss Ray, below, may be later-born or the OCR missed the phrasing — note the census of
younger McWains still active in 1896–98).

Source: `ocr_results/paddle/P81-4721/page_0410-0412.json`

## 2. William (Will) McWain — his full obituary (1896)

> "**Pneumonia Carries Will McWain to the Grave.**
> Last Friday afternoon Will McWain died at his home in this city, of pneumonia, after
> an illness of about two weeks. … The funeral was held Tuesday from the Presbyterian
> church, Rev. J. W. Winder officiating. The church was not large enough to hold all
> the friends who gathered…
>
> William McWain was born November 17, 1865, in the town of Trempealeau. He was a son
> of Mr. and Mrs. John McWain and was the youngest child of a family of five. He grew
> to manhood in this vicinity, and in December, 1890, he was married to Miss Carrie
> Larson. To this union was born one child, now a little girl of four years. He became
> a resident of Galesville about four years ago, buying property near the depot. He
> was an industrious, hard-working young man and had just completed a comfortable home
> when Death knocked at the door. … The parents of the deceased survive him as also do
> his brothers, Everett and Bert. Two sisters died long ago."

- Died: Friday afternoon — the issue is **Friday, March 6, 1896 (Vol. XXII, No. 25)**,
  so death on **February 28, 1896** (note: the same issue as the George Parker death
  notice, which died "the 26th inst."). Funeral Tuesday, March 3, 1896, Presbyterian
  church.
- Wife: **Carrie Larson** (m. December 1890); one daughter, b. ~1892.
- Caution: the OCR interleaves a story about "a mania for firing property … in La
  Crosse" near this obituary — that belongs to a *different* article, not to William.

Source: `ocr_results/paddle/P81-4721/page_0410-0412.json`

## Relationship to the Parkers and Truaxes

- **Mrs. John McWain = born a Parker**, sister of M. B. Parker (Galesville merchant),
  George Parker (d. Feb. 1896, Humbird), and Mrs. William Truax. The Parker siblings
  were born in Vermont (George, at least).
- Therefore William, Everett, and Bert McWain were **nephews of Mrs. William Truax**
  — i.e., the Truax children's first cousins through their mother.
- Note the sad coincidence: **William McWain died of pneumonia on Feb. 28, 1896** —
  the very week his uncle George Parker's death notice ran in the same issue.

## Caveats

- Quotes are verbatim OCR ("McWain" frequently renders as "MeWain"); some columns are
  interleaved. The "firing property" passage near William's obituary belongs to a
  different article.
- The five children of John McWain are only partially identified: William (b. 1865),
  Everett, Bert by name, plus two sisters who died young. Effie, Ray, Lloyd, and
  Gertrude may be children of the brothers rather than of John.
- OCR coverage is still incomplete (P81-4721 nearly done, P81-4722 just started).
