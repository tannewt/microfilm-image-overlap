# The Truaxes of the Galesville Newspaper OCR

A summary of every mention of the **Truax** surname found in the OCR results so far
(`ocr_results/paddle/` — PaddleOCR output over the Galesville, Wisconsin newspaper scans
in `20260826-newspaper-scans/`, plus one hand-verified "golden" transcription in `golden/`).

The scanned volumes are identified as `P81-4720`, `P81-4721`, and `P81-4722`. Based on
internal evidence (McKinley/Bryan election tallies and December 1896 datelines in
`P81-4721`, and "VOL. XX" masthead fragments in `P81-4720`), these papers date to the
mid-1890s. All quotations below are verbatim from the OCR and may contain OCR errors.

**OCR coverage (final):** the corpus is fully OCR'd — all 2,120 pages across the
three rolls (P81-4720: 673 pp., ~1895–96; P81-4721: 658 pp., ~1896–97;
P81-4722: 789 pp., ~1897–1900; rolls are year-mixed bindings, so dates
interleave). No further pages are pending.

## 1. Robert E. Truax — printer, of Langdon, North Dakota

The most frequently mentioned Truax. All evidence points to a young man from Galesville
who worked in the print shop ("force") of the Galesville *Independent*, then moved out
west to Langdon, North Dakota, and returned regularly to visit.

**Summary:** printer, then **foreman of the *Independent* job department for over a
year**, at the Galesville paper; resident of Langdon, N.D. in the mid-1890s; a
"Galesville boy" who still joined hometown events (fair foot race) and visited
Winona, Minnesota often.

**1898–1900:** numerous comings and goings — "Robt. E. Truax journeyed to Winona"
(1898); "Robt. E. Truax returned from Ten[nessee]" (1898); "the Truax had the honor
of 'shaking [hands] with the old boy'" — i.e. meeting **W. J. Bryan**, who "spoke to
50,000 people on that day"; "Robt. E. Truax left Wednesday for [—]"; and a letter
"from Colombia, Tenn., by Robt. E. Truax" (1900). A 1898 list pairs him with River
Falls Normal School people ("W. A. Darhag. Robt. E. Truax."), suggesting he attended
the normal school.

**Key relationship:** Robert E. Truax was a **nephew of Herman L. Vandervort**,
editor and proprietor of The Galesville *Independent* (1891–1895). When Vandervort
died of typhoid fever at Columbia, Tennessee (1898, aged under 30), "the sad news was
received from the home of the deceased at Colombia, Tenn., by Robt. E. Truax, a
nephew of Mr. Vandervort." This explains how the Galesville Truaxes entered the
printing trade.

Sources for the Vandervort items: `ocr_results/paddle/P81-4722/page_0516-0517.json`,
`page_0525-0526.json` (full obituary: "H. L. Vandervort Succumbs to [Typhoid]" — came
to Galesville from Winona in April 1891, bought The Independent with Frank Huntley,
sold January 1895, moved to Winona (J. R. Watkins Co.), then to Columbia, Tenn.,
where he ran a printing plant; left a wife and a little girl).

**Capstone — editor and proprietor (1897):** an affidavit sworn April 8, 1897 for a
paper "published at Langdon, N.D." notes that "at the head of the editorial column …
is noted '**Robt. E. Truax, editor and proprietor.**' We wish Robert all manner of
success in his management" — Robert E. Truax had become **editor and proprietor of
his own newspaper at Langdon, North Dakota**.

Source: `ocr_results/paddle/P81-4722/page_0755-0757.json`

## 2. William Truax, Sr. — old resident of Galesville

From the hand-verified golden transcription of the death notice (image:
`william-truax-sr-death.jpg`):

> **Died in His Bed.**
> William Truax, Sr. whose illness was announced last week, died Monday morning at the
> home of his son, Wyman, near Frenchville. … The funeral was held Wednesday from the
> Presbyterian church in this city, Rev. Winder officiating. The deceased leaves a wife
> and seven children. He was an old resident of Galesville.

**Summary:** elderly longtime Galesville resident; died at his son Wyman's home near
Frenchville; survived by his wife and seven children; funeral at the Galesville
Presbyterian church.

## 8. "Flo Truax" — a fictional character (not a local person)

> "… For née Truax."

Initially mistaken for a marriage notice, but inspection of the page image shows this is
a line of dialogue in a **serialized romance story** in the syndicated fiction supplement
**"The Story Teller"** (illustrated header at the top of the column). The story is
entitled **"A Blessing [in] Heaven"** and is signed "By Marion Mars[h]" with a
copyright line "(Copyright, 189x, by the Auth…)". The heroine is **"luxurious Flo
Truax"**, who lives in a "suburban home in Auburndale"; the male lead is **Fred
Wool(er?)**, who works "the lonely ranch in Texas." The Truax line appears in a
teasing exchange near the bottom of the column ("That's what you're doi… / For née
Truax."); the rest of the line is lost to a page tear at the right edge.

This is a character in fiction, **not** a member of the Galesville Truax family.

Source: `ocr_results/paddle/_ambiguous/page_0507-0508.json` — **now confirmed to be the
`P81-4720` copy** (the story continues from `P81-4720/page_0506-0507.json`, where "Miss
Truax" and "luxurious Flo Truax in her … suburban home in Auburndale" also appear).
The page image is `P81-4720/page_0507-0508.png`, right-most column; the "née Truax" line
sits at bbox ≈ (3940, 2759)–(4162, 2795) in full-resolution page coordinates, with the
story banner just above it (y ≈ 700–1000).

## 11. The Truax family on Galesville village relief (1893–94) — David, Myron, and "W. Truax"

The newly OCR'd **Village of Galesville treasurer's report** (year ending April 24,
1894, plus an "additional report" covering to July 13, 1894) lists repeated poor-relief
payments to a Truax family:

- **"W. Truax, support"** — at least a dozen recurring entries (roughly monthly,
  May 1893 – Feb. 1894), plus "8 W. Truax, support" and "5 W. Truax, support"
- **"David Truax"** — repeatedly; several entries read "David Truax, work on streets"
  (able-bodied relief in exchange for street labor)
- **"Myron Truax,"** support
- **"J. C. Langley, rent of house for Truax family" / "rent for Truax family"** — the
  village paid the family's rent to landlord J. C. Langley at least six times

This is almost certainly the family of **William Truax, Sr.** (#2): "W. Truax" would be
William Sr. himself receiving support, with David and Myron grown sons, before the
family circumstances changed and William Sr. ended his days at son Wyman's farm near
Frenchville (see the golden death notice). Robert E. (#1) may be another son — the
OCR does not say. Note: **Mrs. William Truax was born a Parker**, sister of M. B.
Parker and Mrs. John McWain (see `PARKERS.md` §11 and `MCWAIN.md`).

**Sources:** `ocr_results/paddle/P81-4720/page_0264-0265.json` (13 hits),
`P81-4720/page_0265-0266.json` (16 hits), `P81-4720/page_0644-0644.json` (another
village report: "David Truax", "J. C. Langley, rent 9.00")

## 18. Mrs. William Truax — a sister of M. B. Parker (the Truax–Parker link)

The death notice of **George Parker** of Humbird, Wis. (1896) names the local family
connection:

> "News reached this city yesterday of the death of George Parker, whose demise
> occurred at Humbird, Wis., on the 26th inst. Deceased was well known here and was a
> **brother of M. B. Parker, Mrs. William Truax and Mrs. John McWain**. He was born
> in **Vermont, Sept. 14, 1847**. The remains will be interred tomorrow (Saturday) at
> Centerville."

So **Mrs. William Truax was born a Parker** and was a sister of M. B. Parker (the
Galesville merchant, see `PARKERS.md`). **Family information: her full name was
Mary Esther Parker; Marcellus / M. B. Parker was her brother** (and George Parker of
Humbird, the deceased in this notice, was his brother too). It also means William Truax Sr.'s family and
the Parker family were connected by marriage, and that at least this branch of the
Parker siblings came from **Vermont** — the strongest Vermont link found in the whole
corpus. Which William Truax her husband was (William Sr. or the stage-driving William
of #3a) is not stated; given that George died in 1896 and William Sr. had died in the
mid-1890s, the husband may be the younger William.

Source: `ocr_results/paddle/P81-4721/page_0421-0422.json`

## Caveats

- Quotes are verbatim OCR; the mid-1890s newsprint and two-column layout means columns
  are sometimes interleaved (`_ambiguous/` pages especially), so some readings are
  tentative.
- An earlier, lower-quality OCR pass exists under
  `20260826-newspaper-scans/P81-472*/ocr_results/`; its Truax hits (`Kin Truax`,
  `M. Truax`, `Dave Truax`) are too garbled to add anything reliable.
- The family relationships between Robert E., John, Kin, Lonnie, Mrs. D., and William
  Truax, Sr. are **not** stated in the OCR text — only William Sr./Wyman (father/son)
  is explicit.
